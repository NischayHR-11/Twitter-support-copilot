# Judge-vs-human agreement: notable disagreements

Full numbers: `outputs/human_agreement.json` (generated by
`eval/human_agreement.py`). Headline: **75.8% exact match across all 4
axes, 93.9% within-1-point agreement, mean absolute difference 0.22-0.30
points on a 1-5 scale** depending on axis. Tone is where the judge and
human agree most (94% exact match); correctness and actionability are
where they diverge most (85% and 79%).

The disagreements aren't noise — they cluster around one specific pattern:
**the judge reliably rewards "reply matches the surface pattern of a
retrieved example" even when the reply doesn't actually answer what this
particular customer asked.** Three concrete cases:

- **id 88** (`"Requested a refund 5 days ago and still haven't seen the
  money back"`): the draft reply sends a *new return label*, which
  addresses a completely different scenario (a customer whose return
  label expired) than what was asked (money not received after an
  already-approved refund). Judge scored this 5/5/5/5. Human score:
  2/2/5/3 — the reply is fluent and on-brand but doesn't resolve, or even
  acknowledge, the actual complaint.
- **id 180** (`"can you check order #X AND also why was I charged
  twice"`): a multi-intent message where the draft only answers the
  duplicate-charge half. Judge scored 5/5/5/5; human score 4/3/5/3 — the
  customer still doesn't know what happened to their order.
- **id 2** (`"I want to return the coffee maker... it's not what I
  expected"`): the draft claims a refund "was processed on our end a few
  days ago," presuming a refund request that was never made — the
  customer asked to *start* a return, not check on an existing one. Judge
  scored 5/5/5/5; human score 3/3/5/4.

**Implication:** the LLM-as-judge is trustworthy for surface-level quality
(tone, brand voice, whether *a* resolution was offered) but under-detects
factual mismatches between the specific customer ask and the specific
resolution offered, especially on multi-intent or presumption-heavy
replies. A production eval pipeline should not rely on this judge alone
for the correctness axis — see REPORT.md Section 5 and Section 6.
